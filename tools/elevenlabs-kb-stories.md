# George Kulick: stories and working style

This document adds detail to the main portfolio knowledge base. It is drawn from answers George has given in interviews about his own work. The assistant may use it to answer questions about how George works, examples of problems he has solved, and his strengths.

## Pre-sales and post-sales balance

George's work at LivePerson was about 90 percent post-sales. As a Solutions Architect he also worked the front end of deals: he scoped what the sales team was selling so that commitments were deliverable, wrote the Statements of Work, turned business requirement documents into scope, and then delivered that scope himself. He did not hold an official pre-sales title. At both LivePerson and Quiq his team were the technical people in the company, so when sales had technical questions they came to his team.

George is comfortable on both the pre-sales and post-sales side. What he likes most is being the technical expert on the call.

## Working with account executives

On named accounts such as United Airlines and GoDaddy, George worked with the account team daily. Account executives brought him into new-feature conversations with customers he already knew, because the customer trusted him as the technical voice. Because he wrote the Statement of Work and then executed it himself, what was sold and what was delivered stayed aligned.

## Story: GoDaddy, his most technical customer

GoDaddy was the most technical customer George worked with at LivePerson, and he describes it as his most strategic win. This is the story to tell when someone asks about a difficult situation or a challenge George overcame.

Most customers used LivePerson's standard embedded chat window. GoDaddy rejected that. Their engineers wanted full control, so they decided to build their own chat interface directly on LivePerson's SDK and raw APIs. That made it hard in two ways: it was outside the normal deployment path, and GoDaddy's engineers were sharp enough to find real weaknesses in the APIs and say so.

George became their technical guide. He walked their engineers through the APIs so they could build what they wanted. When they criticised the APIs, he recognised the criticisms were correct and carried them back to LivePerson's product team. That kept GoDaddy's confidence in the platform high through a difficult build. GoDaddy launched on their own interface and stayed with LivePerson.

## Integration work at LivePerson was bespoke

Enterprise integration work at LivePerson was custom for each customer, with little reuse between them. Typical pieces included AWS connections, single sign-on, passing customer data into the conversation, and dashboards fed by webhooks. For hard escalations George worked directly with LivePerson's engineers, for example the specialist who built the single sign-on feature.

## Story: the 10DLC compliance audit tool at Quiq

10DLC is the carrier registration process that businesses in the United States must pass before they can send text messages to customers. At Quiq the process relied on an out-of-date checklist and weeks of auditing by hand.

George replaced it with a Claude project. It reads the customer's terms and conditions, privacy policy and opt-in pages, audits them against the current carrier rules, and produces a one-page report with the supporting evidence pinned to each finding. What had taken weeks of hand-auditing came down to about a week end to end when the customer moved quickly. George has reused the tool on his own since leaving Quiq.

This is George's best example of finding a broken process and rebuilding it with AI.

## Story: using Claude Code for bulk work at Quiq

At Quiq, engineering built an integration layer that gave Claude Code access to a customer tenant's back end. George used it for bulk operations that would otherwise have been done by clicking through the admin screens, for example making changes across 1,200 users and creating 50 new contact points. George was on the usage and feedback side of this tooling. He did not build the integration layer himself.

## Pace: three-week sprints at Quiq

At Quiq both implementation and engineering worked in three-week sprints, and the goal was to go live within a sprint where the customer could keep up. In one case a customer in the middle of an RFP needed a feature that matched what their previous vendor did. Engineering built it that week, and George configured, deployed and tested it before the sprint ended. George is used to a fast pace and high intensity.

## Voice, SMS and telephony experience

Many of George's most recent projects at Quiq involved voice and messaging: standing up voice numbers and SMS, and connecting over SIP and the public phone network. In his experience the slowest part of these projects is usually compliance, meaning getting the customer's brand verified and their opt-in language approved so the carrier application is not rejected.

## AI phone agents George builds

Outside his employment, George builds AI phone agents on ElevenLabs, Twilio and n8n. Scenic City Voice is his business building AI phone agents for local service businesses in the Chattanooga area. He also built the AI front-desk agent for Chattanooga Totes, which answers calls, takes reservations and messages, sends text messages and transfers callers to a person. The assistant on this portfolio site is another agent George set up on ElevenLabs.

## George's top strength

George's top strength is being the trusted technical expert in the room. As an example, when he interviewed at Quiq, one of the interviewers was a former LivePerson colleague who skipped the technical review, saying he probably knew more about the subject than she did.

## A weakness George works on (only for direct questions about weaknesses)

This section answers only a direct question about George's weaknesses, shortcomings or areas for improvement. It is not the answer to a question about a difficult situation, a challenge or something he had to overcome; those are answered with a customer story such as GoDaddy or InSinkErator.

When a problem seems to have no way through, George's default reaction is to put it on the back burner, step away and come back to it. He knows the risk is that deliverables slip if that repeats. So he now says it out loud and escalates early: he states what he has already tried, checks it with AI tools, and then goes to a colleague or manager who has already solved a similar problem.

## What success looks like to George in a new role

George wants to ramp up quickly, contribute like a full member of the team, and see the deals he works on go live without major problems.
